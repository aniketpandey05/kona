What to look at

It is an account row, not a status line. The earlier version gave it four states, and three of them were furniture: “on this device” never changes, “in step” never changes, and mid-sync is a flicker nobody watches.

Only the fourth earned it. 23 allows no notifications, so a sync that has not run since Tuesday has exactly one route to you. The row is quiet the rest of the time precisely so that it is believed when it is not.

Signed out it says Sign in — something you can act on — rather than naming a state you cannot change.

The row exists for one plain reason: signing in has to live somewhere, and so do your account, settings and saving a copy. The amber state is a second job it happens to be well placed for, not the reason it is there.

Clicking it opens: save a copy of everything (first, and saying what it is for — 14 makes it the only backup there is), your devices, settings, what’s new, your email address, and signing out. Nothing else, and none of it while signed out except settings and saving a copy.

Install Kona Its own window and a dock icon, instead of a tab you have to find. Shown only while Kona is running in a tab — like Get the extension, the menu offers what you do not have and nothing you already do (24).
