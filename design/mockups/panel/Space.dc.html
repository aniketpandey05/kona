How an amoeba moves

Freshwater protists, and the mechanics of the pseudopod

An amoeba moves by extending a pseudopod, a temporary projection of the cell body, and then flowing the rest of itself into the space the projection has made.

The mechanism is a change of state in the cytoplasm. The outer layer stiffens into a gel while the interior stays fluid, and pressure from the stiffened region drives the fluid forward.

Feeding uses the same motion. Pseudopods surround a food particle until they meet, enclosing it in a vacuole that is then digested inside the cell.
